## 0. How to read this report

**Scope.** 20 programs across the UK, continental Europe, Canada, East/South-East Asia and India, plus one added program (University of Tübingen MSc Machine Learning) because its lectures are among the best free ML material in existence.

**Sources.** Wherever possible the module lists, syllabi, credit rules and fees come from official university pages (course catalogues, programme specifications, degree regulations, fee schedules) fetched in Oct–Nov 2026. A few fee figures come from reputable third-party aggregators that quote the official numbers; these are flagged. Syllabus content marked *"(recent offering)"* comes from a lecturer's course page from a recent year rather than the official catalogue, and may drift slightly year to year.

**Currency conversions** (mid-market, 18 Nov 2026, [x-rates](https://www.x-rates.com/historical/?amount=1&date=2026-11-18&from=USD)): 1 GBP ≈ 1.32 USD · 1 EUR ≈ 1.10 USD · 1 CHF ≈ 1.19 USD · 1 CAD ≈ 0.70 USD · 1 SGD ≈ 0.78 USD · 1 USD ≈ 1,334 KRW · 1 CNY ≈ 0.15 USD · 1 USD ≈ 93.5 INR. # PART A — UNITED KINGDOM

## 1. University of Oxford — MSc in Advanced Computer Science (incl. AI specialism)

- **Official name:** MSc in Advanced Computer Science; specialist exit titles *MSc Advanced Computer Science (Artificial Intelligence)* and *(Foundations of Computer Science)*.
- **URLs:** [Department page](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/teaching/MSCinCS/) · [Graduate admissions page](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/graduate/courses/msc-advanced-computer-science) · [2025-26 course list](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/teaching/courses/2025-2026/)
- **Duration:** 12 months full-time.
- **Tuition:** 2026-27: Home £18,970 (≈ $25.0k), Overseas £43,730 (≈ $57.7k) ([2026-27 course information sheet](https://www.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/course-information-sheets/2026-27/TM_MF1%20-%20MSc%20in%20Advanced%20Computer%20Science%20-%202026-27%20entry%20-%2028Oct2025_3.pdf)). 2027-28: Home £20,100, Overseas £46,350 (≈ $61.2k) ([Oxford fee table](https://www.ox.ac.uk/students/fees-funding/fees/rates)). Living costs estimated at £1,405–£2,105/month.

### Structure
- Three 8-week terms. Courses run in Michaelmas (autumn) and Hilary (spring) terms, each 16–24 lectures plus classes and sometimes practicals. Students are assessed in **no more than four courses per term**. The **dissertation** is done in Trinity term and over the summer.
- **AI specialism:** students choose from: Advanced Algorithmic Game Theory · Algorithmic Foundations of Collective Decision Making · Computational Learning Theory · Computer Vision · Foundations of Self-Programming Agents · Geometric Deep Learning · Knowledge Representation and Reasoning · Uncertainty in Deep Learning.
- **At most two courses** from a "foundational" list that includes Machine Learning, Computational Game Theory, Computer-Aided Formal Verification, Geometric Modelling, Principles of Programming Languages, Quantum Information.
- **Assessment** is either a written exam (Trinity term) or a take-home mini-project (Christmas/Easter vacation). ML-relevant: Uncertainty in DL → mini-project; Geometric DL → mini-project; Computational Learning Theory → exam; Computer Vision → exam; KRR → exam.

### Key modules and syllabi

**Machine Learning** (Michaelmas, 20 lectures; Seth Flaxman, 2025-26) — [course page](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/teaching/courses/2025-2026/ml/)
- Paradigms of ML; linear prediction and regression
- Maximum likelihood, MAP, Bayesian ML
- Regularisation, generalisation, cross-validation
- Basics of optimisation
- Linear classification, logistic regression, naïve Bayes
- Support vector machines; kernel methods
- Neural networks and backpropagation; CNNs; RNNs
- Unsupervised learning: clustering, k-means; dimensionality reduction, PCA
- *Reading:* Bishop *PRML*; Goodfellow et al. *Deep Learning*; Murphy *MLaPP*; Hastie et al. *ESL*.
- *Prerequisites:* Continuous Mathematics, Linear Algebra, Probability, Design and Analysis of Algorithms.

**Uncertainty in Deep Learning** (Michaelmas 2026, 20 lectures; Yarin Gal) — [course page](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/teaching/courses/2026-2027/UDL/). Lecture-level synopsis:
1. Introduction — ML in the real world and when we can't trust it; sources of error and uncertainty; what "Bayesian deep learning" is
2. Bayesian probability theory as the language of uncertainty — betting games, belief as willingness to wager, deriving the laws of probability from rational beliefs
3. Bayesian probabilistic modelling — generative story, likelihood as a function of parameters, the posterior
4. Bayesian modelling of functions — linear regression → basis-function regression → parametrised basis functions → hierarchies of basis functions (neural networks) seen probabilistically
5. Uncertainty over functions — aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty
6. Approximate inference — variational inference, KL properties (incl. continuous RVs), the evidence objective and its competing tensions
7. Mathematical tools — Monte Carlo integration, integral derivatives, the reparametrisation trick
8. Stochastic VI in deep models; Bayesian neural networks
9–10. Inference in real-world deep models — stochastic regularisation (dropout) as approximate inference, MC dropout, visualising BNNs
11–12. Classification BNNs; information-theoretic uncertainty (predictive entropy, mutual information)
13. Active learning (acquisition functions; MNIST example; batch active learning)
14. Applications: Galaxy Zoo, exoplanet retrieval, diabetic retinopathy; calibration metrics and benchmarks
15. Autonomous driving: segmentation uncertainty, Bayesian decision theory, loss-calibrated BNNs, distribution shift
- *Prereqs:* ML course, multivariate calculus/probability, Python/PyTorch. *Reading:* Murphy *PML 1 & 2* (free), Goodfellow et al.

**Geometric Deep Learning** (Hilary, 20 lectures; Michael Bronstein) — [course page](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/teaching/courses/2025-2026/geodl/) · [free GDL book + slides](https://geometricdeeplearning.com/book/)
- Symmetry, invariance and equivariance as the blueprint for deep learning; deriving CNNs, GNNs, Transformers, DeepSets and LSTMs from first principles
- Lectures map onto the book: introduction (L1–2); learning in high dimension (L3); geometric priors (L4–5); graphs (L7–8); grids (L9–10); groups and homogeneous spaces (L11); geometric graphs and geometric GNNs (L12)
- Background maths: vector calculus, differential geometry, group/representation theory, spectral and graph theory.

**Computational Learning Theory** (Michaelmas 2026, 24 lectures; Varun Kanade) — [course page](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/teaching/courses/2026-2027/clt/) · [lecturer page](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/varun.kanade/teaching/CLT-MT2026/)
- Background: probability, concentration inequalities, computational complexity
- PAC learning, sample complexity, computational complexity; proper vs improper learning
- Growth function, VC dimension, sample-complexity lower bounds
- Membership queries and Angluin's algorithm
- Boosting, weak learning, AdaBoost, margin bounds
- Learning with noise, Statistical Query learning, agnostic learning; learning parities with noise, juntas
- Complexity-theoretic and cryptographic hardness of learning
- Online learning (perceptron, winnow), learning from expert advice, multi-armed bandits
- Optional: learning distributions, privacy-preserving learning, adaptive data analysis
- *Syllabus taken from the [2018-19 page](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/teaching/courses/2018-2019/clt/); the 2026-27 description is unchanged in spirit.*

Other ML-adjacent department courses (2025-26 list): Computer Vision, Knowledge Representation & Reasoning, Probabilistic Model Checking (Kwiatkowska: DTMCs, MDPs, PCTL/LTL/CSL, PRISM), Physics-Informed Neural Networks, Deep Learning in Healthcare, Computational Medicine.

**Prerequisites (program):** first-class/strong 2:1 degree in CS or mathematics; the ML courses assume linear algebra, multivariate calculus, probability, algorithms and Python.

**Notable assessment:** mini-projects for UDL and GDL (implementation plus a research-style report), proof-based exam for CLT, and a research dissertation.

### Free Oxford material
- **Deep Learning at Oxford 2015** — Nando de Freitas, 16 lectures: linear models, maximum likelihood and information, regularisation, optimisation, logistic regression/backprop in Torch, CNNs, max-margin learning and memory networks, RNNs/LSTMs, guest lectures by Alex Graves and Karol Gregor (VAEs), deep RL — [YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLE6Wd9FR--EfW8dtjAuPoTuPcqmOV53Fu) · [channel](https://www.youtube.com/@ProfNandoDF)
- **Oxford Deep NLP 2017** (Phil Blunsom with DeepMind; about 13 lectures plus practicals) — [lectures and video links](https://github.com/oxford-cs-deepnlp-2017/lectures)
- **Geometric Deep Learning** — free book chapters and Oxford slides: [geometricdeeplearning.com](https://geometricdeeplearning.com/book/)
- Recent UDL, ML and CLT lectures are not publicly recorded. Use the reading lists (Murphy PML1/PML2 are free; Kanade posts notes on his course page).

## 2. University of Cambridge — MPhil in Machine Learning and Machine Intelligence (MLMI)

- **URLs:** [MLMI site](https://www.mlmi.eng.cam.ac.uk/) · [Indicative course structure](https://www.mlmi.eng.cam.ac.uk/indicative-outline-course-structure) · [Postgraduate course directory / finance](https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/courses/directory/egegmpmsl/finance)

### Key modules and syllabi

**MLMI1 Introduction to Machine Learning** — [page](https://www.mlmi.eng.cam.ac.uk/mlmi-1-introduction-machine-learning). An accelerated foundation module:
- Statistical inference: maximum likelihood vs Bayesian, with strengths and weaknesses of each
- Regression, classification, clustering and sequence modelling, using linear algebra (eigen-decomposition), multivariate calculus and calculus of variations
- Optimisation and dynamic programming: gradient and coordinate descent, EM, belief propagation, Kalman filter and Viterbi decoding

**4F13 Probabilistic Machine Learning** (14 lectures, 3 courseworks, no exam; 2025 lecturers Hong Ge, George Cantwell, Ayush Tewari) — [course page](https://mlg.eng.cam.ac.uk/teaching/4f13/2526/). Organised around three case studies:
- *Intro (2L):* modelling data; linear-in-the-parameters regression; likelihood and noise; probability fundamentals; Bayesian inference and prediction with finite regression models; marginal likelihood
- *Gaussian processes (4L):* parameters vs functions; GP definition and sampling; GP marginal likelihood and hyperparameters; linear models ↔ GPs; finite vs infinite models; covariance functions (GPy)
- *Probabilistic ranking — TrueSkill (5L):* Gibbs sampling; factor graphs; message passing; moment matching (EP-style approximation)
- *Modelling document collections:* categorical/multinomial models; mixture models and EM; collapsed Gibbs sampling; Latent Dirichlet Allocation
- **Coursework:** (1) GP regression in GPy/Colab; (2) TrueSkill probabilistic ranking ([GitHub](https://github.com/gcant/4F13-CW2)); (3) LDA on the KOS document data.
- *Reading:* Murphy, Barber *BRML* (free), Bishop *PRML*, MacKay *ITILA* (free). Maths cribsheet and Matrix Cookbook recommended.

**4F10 Deep Learning and Structured Data** (14L + 2 examples classes; Mark Gales, J. M. Hernández-Lobato; 100% exam) — [2025-26 syllabus](https://teaching.eng.cam.ac.uk/content/engineering-tripos-part-iib-4f10-deep-learning-structured-data-2025-26-0)
- Latent-variable and sequence models (2L): GMMs, factor analysis, HMMs, EM
- Deep learning (3L): generative vs discriminative deep models, activations, CNNs, mixture-density networks, first/second-order optimisation, adaptive learning rates, initialisation
- Deep learning for sequences (2L): RNNs, bidirectional RNNs, Transformers (encoder-only, encoder-decoder, decoder-only), aligning LLMs, chain-of-thought
- Ensembles and distillation (1L): deep ensembles, model combination, distillation
- SVMs (2L): max-margin, non-separable data, kernels, multi-class SVMs
- Kernels over structured data (1L): string, graph and Fisher kernels
- Earlier editions also covered graphical models, CRFs, VAEs/GANs, structured SVMs, RVMs and non-parametric methods (trees, Parzen windows, kNN, random forests, boosting).

**4M24 Computational Statistics and Machine Learning** (Mark Girolami; 75% exam / 25% coursework; lectures recorded) — [syllabus](https://teaching.eng.cam.ac.uk/content/engineering-tripos-part-iib-4m24-computational-statistics-and-machine-learning-2025-26). Lecture by lecture:
1–2. Monte Carlo: LLN/CLT for MC estimators, importance sampling, control variates
3–4. Lebesgue integral and measure; Radon–Nikodym; measure-theoretic probability
5–6. MCMC: Metropolis–Hastings, Gibbs
7–9. Vector, metric, Banach, Hilbert and Sobolev spaces; RKHS
10. Gaussian measures in Hilbert space; Bayes' rule in function space (GPs)
11. MCMC in Hilbert space (dimension-invariant kernels)
12–13. Langevin dynamics
14. Parallel tempering

**MLMI4 Advanced Machine Learning** (Lent, full) — [page](https://www.mlmi.eng.cam.ac.uk/mlmi-4-advanced-machine-learning). Advanced topics with the goal of reading and implementing current research. It is assessed largely through paper-replication group projects; past posters include "Towards a Neural Statistician" and "Doubly Stochastic VI for Deep GPs" ([course highlights](https://www.mlmi.eng.cam.ac.uk/course-highlights-2019-2020)).

**MLMI7 Reinforcement Learning and Decision Making** (Lent, half) — sequential decision-making under uncertainty, the RL ↔ optimal control connection, and the exploration–exploitation trade-off.

**Related Cambridge (Computer Lab, MPhil ACS) courses useful to replicate:** L173 *Probabilistic Machine Learning* (Damon Wischik, 2026-27: deep learning as probabilistic modelling, variational/adversarial/energy-based bounds, uncertainty, causal deep learning, transformers ↔ GPs — [page](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/teaching/2627/L173/)); L65 *Geometric Deep Learning* (slides on the GDL site); L48 *ML and the Physical World*.

**Prerequisites:** First-class degree in engineering, maths, physics or CS. Strong calculus, linear algebra, probability/statistics and programming. 4F13 explicitly requires reviewing a maths cribsheet.

### Free Cambridge material
- **David MacKay — Information Theory, Pattern Recognition and Neural Networks** (16 lectures, 2012): entropy and compression, noisy-channel coding, Bayesian inference, clustering, Monte Carlo (importance, rejection, Gibbs, Metropolis, slice, HMC), variational methods, neural networks — [official videos and slides](https://www.inference.org.uk/itprnn_lectures/) · [YouTube mirror (15 of 16)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLN3p8NUNcClDu1hc2m5cVp8FOEmuF3vRy). The textbook *ITILA* is free.
- **4F13 slides, coursework and code** — [mlg.eng.cam.ac.uk/teaching/4f13](https://mlg.eng.cam.ac.uk/teaching/4f13/2526/). The 2021 site has the full chunked slide set.
- Free texts used: Rasmussen & Williams *GPML*, Barber *BRML*, MacKay *ITILA*.

### MSc Artificial Intelligence — structure (2026-27)
- **Autumn:** compulsory Machine Learning (COMP70050) and Python Programming (COMP70053), plus electives.
- **Spring:** more electives plus the **MSc AI Software Engineering Group Project** (COMP70079, 10 ECTS; often industry-proposed; demo and presentation at the start of summer).
- **May–September:** **Individual Project** (COMP70078, 35 ECTS, core) or an AI internship, with dissertation and oral presentation.
- **Electives:** 7 total, with 6–7 from Group S (AI/ML) and at most 1 from Group O.
  - **Group S:** Mathematics for ML (70015), Reinforcement Learning (70028), Logic & Symbolic AI (70077), Computational Neurodynamics (70100), Human–Robot Interaction (70101), Computer Vision (70110), Computational Optimisation (70007), Generative AI (70010), ML for Imaging (70014), NLP (70016), Probabilistic Inference (70019), Formal Methods for Safe AI (70031), Robot Learning (70067), Causality & Explanation in AI (70076), Logic-Based Learning (70093), Software Engineering for ML Systems (70102), Deep Graph-Based Learning (70105), Non-Euclidean Methods in ML (70112), World Models (70116), Generative Methods in AI for Science (70118), AI Ventures (BUSI70233).
  - **Group O:** Computational Finance, Distributed Ledgers, Quantum Computing, Statistical Information Theory (70103), Automated Software Engineering.
- In 2025-26 the compulsory set also included Introduction to Symbolic AI and Ethics, Fairness & Explanation in AI ([2025-26 spec](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/study/programme-specifications/computing/2526/G5T1-MSc-Artificial-Intelligence-2025-26.pdf); [degree-structure page](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/teaching/dyn-degree-struc/generator/webdgrstr/pai/5)).

### MSc Computing (AI & ML) — structure
- **Core:** Individual Project and Mathematics for Machine Learning.
- **Options:** choose 5–8 modules from Group 1 and up to 3 from Group 2.
- **Group 1:** Logic-Based Learning, Computational Optimisation, Deep Learning, ML for Imaging, NLP, Probabilistic Inference, Reinforcement Learning, Formal Methods for Safe AI, Prolog, Introduction to ML, Computer Vision, Robot Learning, Computational Neurodynamics, Deep Graph-Based Learning, HRI, Methods & Tools in Theory of Computing, Statistical Information Theory, SE for ML Systems, Non-Euclidean Methods in ML, ML Systems & Hardware.

### Key module syllabi
**COMP70015 Mathematics for Machine Learning** (Autumn; historically Marc Deisenroth and Stefanos Zafeiriou; [2019-20 page](https://www.deisenroth.cc/teaching/2019-20/mathematics-for-machine-learning/)). Organised around three worked models:
1. *Bayesian linear regression:* vector calculus (partial derivatives, chain rule, Jacobians), multivariate Gaussian, Bayes' theorem, conjugate priors, gradient descent, model selection, cross-validation, MLE/MAP, Bayesian integration, graphical-model notation
2. *Probabilistic PCA:* eigenvalues, determinants, basis change, SVD, Gram–Schmidt, rotations, projections
3. *SVMs:* constrained optimisation, Lagrange multipliers
- Recent offerings add Monte Carlo estimation, concentration inequalities, gradient-descent convergence and validation/test error estimation.
- **Free equivalents:** the book *Mathematics for Machine Learning* (Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong) at [mml-book.github.io](https://mml-book.github.io/), and Imperial's Coursera [Mathematics for ML specialisation](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/mathematics-machine-learning) (linear algebra, multivariate calculus, PCA; free to audit).

**COMP70050 (Introduction to) Machine Learning** (recent offering): instance-based learning (k-NN), decision trees, evaluation methodology (cross-validation, confusion matrices, statistical significance), artificial neural networks and backprop, unsupervised learning (k-means, GMM/EM), evolutionary algorithms. Coursework builds a decision tree and a small neural-network library from scratch in NumPy.

**COMP70010 Deep Learning** (Bernhard Kainz et al.; flipped classroom; [course page](http://wp.doc.ic.ac.uk/bkainz/teaching/70010-deep-learning-from-2023/)): curse of dimensionality and convolutions; CNN building blocks; activation and loss functions; architectures (LeNet → AlexNet → VGG → Inception → BatchNorm → ResNet/DenseNet); U-Net; data augmentation; generative models (VAEs, GANs, advances, diffusion); RNN basics and applications; attention and Transformers; foundation models (tokenisation, evaluation, scaling); GNNs/GCNs (guest lecture by Bronstein). Two assessed coursework tasks on the LabTS system, plus quizzes. Many lectures have linked YouTube videos.

**COMP70019 Probabilistic Inference** (Mark van der Wilk; [2023 plan](https://github.com/markvdw/probinf-spring-2023)):
- Building probabilistic models; graphical models
- Priors on functions; from linear models to GPs; GPs; model selection; marginal likelihood; GP limitations
- Decision theory; Bayesian optimisation
- Conjugate vs non-conjugate models; Bayesian logistic regression; Monte Carlo; MCMC
- Variational inference; stochastic and amortised VI; VAEs
- Diffusion models

## S2. What is distinctive, by region and school

**UK (Cambridge, UCL, Oxford, Edinburgh): probabilistic and Bayesian ML.**
- Gaussian processes, approximate inference (variational, EP, MCMC including HMC/SMC, Langevin), graphical models, Bayesian deep learning, kernel methods/RKHS and information theory carry far more weight here than anywhere else.
- Examples: Cambridge 4F13 teaches GPs, TrueSkill (factor graphs and EP) and LDA through coursework. 4M24 goes to Gaussian measures on Hilbert spaces. UCL's Gatsby sequence (COMP0086 → COMP0085) and Gretton's kernel module (MMD/HSIC) are unique. Oxford has Gal's *Uncertainty in DL* and a proof-based *Computational Learning Theory*. Edinburgh's PML is "pen-and-paper" derivation.
- The MacKay heritage (information theory ↔ inference) runs through Cambridge.
- Programs are 1-year intensive, with a research dissertation as the capstone. Cambridge MLMI also has distinctive **speech** depth (MLMI2/5/6/8), reflecting the Gales/Woodland/Young lab.

**Switzerland (ETH, EPFL): optimisation and theory with proofs.**
- Full 8–10-ECTS courses devoted to *optimisation for ML/DS* with convergence proofs and lower bounds, plus learning theory (PAC, NTK, double descent) and large-scale systems.
- ETH's PAI unifies Bayesian ML with active learning, Bayesian optimisation and model-based RL. Its DL course is explicitly mathematical.
- Very low fees, 2-year programs, and heavy project work (ETH DS Lab, EPFL ML4Science projects).

**Netherlands (UvA): deep learning practice plus geometric/equivariant DL.**
- Seven-course mandatory core covering ML, DL, CV, NLP, IR, RL and FACT, so ethics is compulsory.
- A uniquely strong **geometric deep learning** strand (group-equivariant and steerable CNNs, from the Welling/Cohen/Bekkers school), deep probabilistic models and an exceptional open-source tutorial ecosystem (PyTorch and JAX, including parallelism at scale).

**Germany (Tübingen, TUM): probabilistic numerics, statistical learning theory, vision and robotics.**
- Tübingen's mandatory triad: *Statistical ML* (von Luxburg; consistency, RKHS, learning theory), *Probabilistic ML* (Hennig/Macke) and *Deep Learning* (Geiger/Bordt). *Numerics of ML* is unique.
- TUM is vision- and robotics-centric: multiple-view geometry, SLAM, 3D DL and robot dynamics, with ML (Günnemann) as a pillar.

**Canada (Toronto, Montréal/Mila): deep learning lineage plus industry pipeline.**
- Representation learning (Hinton/Bengio/Courville), with courses built around the *Deep Learning* textbook.
- Professional master's degrees are built around **paid industrial internships** (8 months at MScAC, 6 months at Mila) plus communication and entrepreneurship modules rather than a research thesis.

**Asia (NUS, KAIST, Tsinghua): breadth with governance or theory.**
- **NUS** structures AI into four sub-areas, including a compulsory-option *AI Governance* area.
- **KAIST** has one of the deepest elective catalogues anywhere: DL theory, ML theory, stochastic processes, information theory, Bayesian DL, Bayesian nonparametrics, LLMs, embodied AI. Most international students are fully funded.
- **Tsinghua** stresses probabilistic generative modelling and Bayesian nonparametrics (Jun Zhu's group) and theory (Andrew Yao's ATCS course).

**India (IISc, IITs, IIIT-H): mathematical foundations as hardcore.**
- Programs front-load **linear algebra, probability/stochastic processes, optimisation and DSA** as compulsory courses (IISc Pool A, IITH year 1, IIIT-H semester 1) before ML specialisation.
- A classical **pattern-recognition tradition** (Duda–Hart, Bayes decision theory, Sastry's PR course) and **stochastic-approximation RL** (Bhatnagar's DP-centred RL, Ravindran's bandits-to-POMDPs course).
- Long research projects (IISc 25 credits; IITH 24 credits of thesis across 3 stages), extremely low fees, and a huge free NPTEL corpus.
- The IITM online ladder is a fully replicable, video-complete BS → M.Tech AI curriculum.

## S3. Implication for the self-study platform: a reconstructed "consensus master's"

Taking the intersection of these curricula and weighting by depth suggests a ~2-year, ~120-ECTS-equivalent structure:

1. **Mathematical foundations (≈ 20 ECTS):** linear algebra for ML (IITH AI5110 + Strang + MML book); probability and stochastic processes (IITH AI5030 / IISc E1 222); multivariate calculus; convex optimisation (Boyd + ETH ODS / EPFL OptML notes). Free lectures: Tübingen *Math for ML*, Geiger *Math for DL*, IITM MLF, Imperial MML (Coursera).
2. **Core ML (≈ 10 ECTS):** UvA ML1 / TUM IN2064 / EPFL CS-433 / Tübingen SML (proof-level option: UCL COMP0078 topics).
3. **Probabilistic ML and inference (≈ 10 ECTS):** Cambridge 4F13 (GPs, TrueSkill, LDA courseworks), Edinburgh PML, UCL COMP0086/0085, Tübingen PML (Hennig), ETH PAI; MacKay ITILA for information theory.
4. **Deep learning (≈ 10 ECTS):** UvA DL1 + Lippe notebooks, ETH DL topics, Fleuret DLC, DeepMind×UCL 2020, Khapra NPTEL; then DL2-level generative models (VAE/flows/diffusion).
5. **Reinforcement learning (≈ 6–8 ECTS):** Silver (UCL 2015) → DeepMind×UCL RL 2021 → Ravindran NPTEL (bandits, POMDPs, hierarchical RL) → ETH PAI part II (model-based, safe exploration).
6. **Learning theory (≈ 6 ECTS):** Oxford CLT syllabus, EPFL CS-526, Shalev-Shwartz & Ben-David, Sastry NPTEL modules 7.
7. **Specialisations (≈ 20–30 ECTS):** NLP/LLMs (EPFL CS-552, ETH LLMs, Oxford Deep NLP 2017), vision (TUM MVG, NPTEL DL4CV), geometric DL (Oxford/Cambridge GDL book, UvA DL2), Bayesian DL (Oxford UDL syllabus), kernels (UCL COMP0083 syllabus), ethics/FACT (UvA, Cambridge MLMI3).
8. **Capstones:** replicate famous courseworks — Cambridge 4F13 CW1–3, EPFL CS-433 Projects 1–2, Edinburgh MLP (build a NumPy DL framework), Imperial 70050 (decision tree + NN library from scratch), Cambridge MLMI4-style paper replication, and a thesis-scale research project (UK-style 3-month or EU-style 6-month).

## S5. Caveats and gaps

- **Fees change annually.** Cambridge overseas and Edinburgh fees came via aggregators that cite official data; confirm on the official calculators before publishing. Toronto's MScAC total depends on session billing.
- **Syllabi marked "recent offering"** (Imperial 70050/70028, NUS CS5340/5446, Tübingen PML detail) come from recent lecturer pages or course histories rather than the 2026-27 catalogue.
- **Restricted recordings:** several flagship modules (Cambridge 4F13/MLMI4, Oxford UDL/CLT, UCL Gatsby modules, NUS, KAIST, Tsinghua) have no current public video. The platform should pair their official syllabi with the free textbooks and the open lecture series listed above, which collectively cover essentially all their topics.
- **DeepMind × UCL RL 2021 playlist** is labelled "Deep Learning Lecture Series 2021" on YouTube. Some Tübingen and MediaSpace links point to channel or course pages rather than individual playlists.
